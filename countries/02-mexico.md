# Mexico — industrial pump procurement

Mexico is the second-largest LATAM industrial pump market. The dominant
demand drivers are oil & gas (PEMEX and private operators post-2013
energy reform), automotive supply chain, food and beverage processing,
and large-scale infrastructure (water utilities and irrigation).

## 1. Standards and certifications — NOM

Mexican mandatory product standards are issued under the **NOM** (Norma
Oficial Mexicana) framework. NOM compliance is generally required for
products sold in Mexico; certification is performed by accredited bodies
(EMA — Entidad Mexicana de Acreditación).

Key NOMs for pumps and adjacent equipment:

| NOM | Scope |
|-----|-------|
| NOM-001-SEDE | Electrical installations (referenced for pump electrical drives) |
| NOM-022-STPS | Hazardous-area workplace electrical (Ex-rated equipment) |
| NOM-029-STPS | Maintenance of electrical installations |
| NOM-085-SEMARNAT | Air emissions (relevant to diesel-driven pumps) |
| NOM-EM-007-CONAGUA | Drinking-water-system equipment (relevant to potable-water pumps) |

**Voluntary standards** widely used for industrial pumps: API 610,
API 676, API 682, ANSI/HI 14.6 — same as international practice.

For oil & gas duty: PEMEX qualified-vendor registry (similar to CRCC
Petrobras). Major private operators also maintain qualified-vendor
lists; getting on them takes 6-18 months and substantial documentation.

## 2. Financing — NAFIN and FIRA

**NAFIN** (Nacional Financiera) is the federal development bank.
Operates several credit lines for capital equipment:

- **Crédito PYME** (small-medium enterprise credit) — short to medium term
- **Crédito Empresarial** — long-term capital expenditure financing
- **Cadenas Productivas** — supply-chain financing for known-good
  procurement programs

Typical interest rates (2026): TIIE + 3-7% (currently ~13-17% nominal).
Generally less favorable than BNDES Finame in Brazil.

**FIRA** (Fideicomisos Instituidos en Relación con la Agricultura) is
agricultural-sector focused but relevant for irrigation pump financing.

**USMCA / T-MEC local-content rules**: USMCA replaces NAFTA. For pumps
sold to USMCA-eligible OEMs (typically automotive or aerospace
suppliers), local-content thresholds matter for tariff exemption.
Industrial pumps for non-USMCA end-use are not affected.

## 3. Import duties and taxes

| Tax / duty | Typical rate (industrial pumps) |
|------------|--------------------------------|
| Import duty (general) | 10-15% under standard tariff schedule |
| USMCA-origin from US/Canada | 0% if rules of origin met |
| IVA (VAT) | 16% on landed value |
| DTA (customs processing fee) | 0.8% |

Effective tax burden on imported industrial pumps from non-USMCA
origin: typically **27-32%** on top of FOB cost. Imports from US/Canada
under USMCA typically **17-18%** total.

This makes Mexico significantly more import-friendly than Brazil for
non-Brazilian pumps.

## 4. PEMEX and energy-sector procurement

Petróleos Mexicanos (PEMEX) operates a qualified-vendor system similar
to Petrobras CRCC. Vendors must:

- Register through the PEMEX Padrón de Proveedores
- Demonstrate financial capacity, technical capability, and
  manufacturing/service infrastructure
- Pass periodic renewals (typically biennial)
- Submit specific qualifications per equipment family

After the 2013-2014 energy reform, private oil-and-gas operators
(Eni, Repsol, Talos Energy, Murphy, BP, Shell, etc.) operate their own
vendor registries — typically less burdensome than PEMEX but requiring
their own documentation.

## 5. Documentation language

Mexican regulatory documentation must be in **Spanish**. Industrial
practice for technical documentation:

- O&M manuals: Spanish mandatory for end-user operation
- Test certificates: Spanish OR English (English accepted in oil-and-gas
  practice)
- Schematic drawings: typically Spanish nomenclature, dimensions in
  metric (industrial) or imperial (oil-and-gas)

Translation cost less than Brazil because the regulatory tolerance for
English documentation is greater in Mexican private-sector practice.

## 6. Vendor pool — typical bidders

For industrial pumps in Mexico:

- **Ruhrpumpen** (Monterrey) — Mexican manufacturer, large-pump focus, oil & gas heavy
- **Goulds Pumps** (US, with Mexican distribution) — broad industrial range
- **Flowserve México** (Tijuana) — assembly + distribution
- **Sulzer México** (San Luis Potosí) — assembly
- **Grundfos México** — distribution + service
- **WEG Pumps** (Brazilian manufacturer with Mexican presence)
- **FB Bombas** (Brazilian manufacturer, exports to Mexican market with documentation in Spanish)

For specialty pumps (positive-displacement, sanitary, slurry): typically
imported from US or European specialty manufacturers.

## 7. Logistics and infrastructure

Major ports: Veracruz, Manzanillo, Lázaro Cárdenas. Veracruz is the
primary Atlantic port handling European and Brazilian imports;
Manzanillo and Lázaro Cárdenas handle Asian imports. Customs clearance
typically 2-4 weeks for standard industrial equipment.

Inland: extensive highway network connects industrial corridors
(Bajío, Norte, Centro). Rail is more available than in Brazil but
specialized for bulk commodities.

## 8. Currency and contract structure

Most large Mexican industrial procurement is in USD or with USD-pegged
clauses to manage peso volatility. Smaller projects may be MXN-only.

For contracts > USD 500.000, multi-currency clauses are common:
equipment in USD, services in MXN, with a stated exchange-rate basis
date and adjustment mechanism for delays.

## 9. Procurement timeline — typical Mexican project

| Stage | Typical duration |
|-------|-----------------|
| RFQ preparation | 2-4 weeks |
| Bidding period | 3-4 weeks |
| Bid evaluation + award | 3-6 weeks (longer if PEMEX vendor) |
| Manufacturing — local | 10-14 weeks |
| Manufacturing — USMCA import | 12-16 weeks |
| Manufacturing — non-USMCA import (incl. Brazil) | 16-22 weeks |
| FAT + shipment | 2-3 weeks |
| Customs + inland | 3-5 weeks |
| Site install + commission | 4-6 weeks |

USMCA imports are typically **4-6 weeks faster** than non-USMCA imports
because of streamlined customs.
