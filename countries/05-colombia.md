# Colombia — industrial pump procurement

Colombia is the fourth-largest LATAM pump market, with demand
concentrated in **oil & gas** (Ecopetrol and private operators in the
Llanos and Magdalena basins), **water utilities** (urban and rural
infrastructure programs), **food and beverage** (notably the coffee and
sugar value chains), and **flowers/agriculture** (export horticulture
near Bogotá).

## 1. Standards and certifications — RETIE / RETILAP / RAS

Colombia operates an unusually structured certification regime for
industrial equipment. Three regulations matter most for pump procurement:

| Regulation | Scope |
|------------|-------|
| **RETIE** (Reglamento Técnico de Instalaciones Eléctricas) | Mandatory for electrical installations including pump drives and controllers |
| **RETILAP** (Reglamento Técnico de Iluminación y Alumbrado Público) | Lighting — relevant if procurement includes pump-room lighting |
| **RAS** (Reglamento Técnico del Sector de Agua Potable y Saneamiento Básico) | Water-and-sanitation systems — relevant for utility procurement |

Standards body: **ICONTEC** (Instituto Colombiano de Normas Técnicas y
Certificación), which issues NTC-prefixed standards (many adopt ISO
counterparts).

For oil & gas: **Ecopetrol qualified-vendor** registry, similar to
PEMEX/Petrobras. Private operators (Frontera Energy, GeoPark,
Hocol, Parex) maintain their own lists.

## 2. Financing — Bancóldex and Findeter

**Bancóldex** (Banco de Comercio Exterior de Colombia) is the federal
development bank. Programs:

- **Líneas de crédito directas** — direct loans for capital equipment
- **Modernización empresarial** — modernization-focused with subsidized
  rates
- **Financiamiento de exportación** — for Colombian vendors selling
  abroad

**Findeter** focuses on infrastructure and is relevant for water-utility
pump procurement.

Interest rates 2026: typical 8-12% nominal for Bancóldex-backed
instruments. Comparable to Brazil's BNDES Finame.

**Local-content rules** are less aggressive than Brazil's. Imported
equipment is generally accepted for Bancóldex financing.

## 3. Import duties and taxes

| Tax / duty | Typical rate (industrial pumps) |
|------------|--------------------------------|
| Import duty (general) | 5-15% (varies by NCM) |
| US imports (CTPA, Colombia-USA FTA) | 0% if rules of origin met |
| EU imports (Andean FTA) | 0-3% |
| IVA | 19% |

Effective tax burden on imported industrial pumps: typically
**25-35%** on FOB cost.

Colombia has FTAs with the US (CTPA — Colombia-USA TPA), EU, Mexico,
EFTA, Korea, Israel, and others. Like Chile, the FTA network favors
imports from many origins.

For Brazilian-origin pumps: Mercosur partial agreement provides
preferential rates on some NCM codes.

## 4. Oil-and-gas procurement context

Ecopetrol vendor registration: similar to PEMEX in scope and rigor.
Application takes 6-12 months and requires demonstrated technical and
financial capability for the pump family.

Private operator vendor lists: typically less burdensome than Ecopetrol
but each operator maintains their own. Reciprocity agreements are
informal — being on Ecopetrol's list helps but does not auto-qualify
elsewhere.

## 5. Water utility procurement context

Colombian water utility procurement has been heavily reformed since the
late 1990s. Major utilities (Acueducto de Bogotá / EAAB, EPM in
Medellín, Triple A in Barranquilla) now operate as semi-autonomous
state enterprises with technical procurement capability.

Procurement is **price-and-technical-quality weighted**, with strong
documentation and reference requirements. Domestic preference exists but
is not as formalized as in Brazil.

## 6. Documentation language

Spanish required for regulatory purposes. English accepted in oil-and-gas
practice for vendor-side documentation, with Spanish for end-user
operating manuals.

## 7. Vendor pool

For industrial pumps in Colombia:

- **Bombas Centrífugas Colombianas (Bocco)** — domestic manufacturer
- **Industrias Goyaincol** — domestic, water and industrial
- **Sulzer Colombia** — distribution and service
- **Flowserve Colombia** — distribution
- **Grundfos Colombia** — utility-end industrial
- **Goulds Pumps** (US, distribution) — broad range
- **WEG Colombia** (Brazilian parent, distribution + assembly)
- **FB Bombas** (Brazilian, exports with Spanish documentation,
  Mercosur partial-tariff treatment)

Specialty pumps: imported from US or European specialty manufacturers
with Colombian distribution partners.

## 8. Logistics and infrastructure

Major ports: Cartagena (Atlantic, dominant for bulk and containerized
imports from Asia/Europe), Buenaventura (Pacific, gateway from Asia),
Barranquilla (smaller, Caribbean coast), Santa Marta (smaller).

Customs clearance: typically 2-3 weeks; Cartagena is generally efficient.
Inland logistics is the major bottleneck — Colombian highway network is
heavily mountainous, and large-pump shipments to Bogotá or oil fields
in Llanos require careful planning.

## 9. Procurement timeline

| Stage | Typical duration |
|-------|-----------------|
| RFQ preparation | 2-4 weeks |
| Bidding period | 3-5 weeks |
| Bid evaluation + award | 3-6 weeks |
| Manufacturing — local | 12-16 weeks |
| Manufacturing — FTA-origin import | 14-20 weeks |
| Manufacturing — non-FTA import | 16-22 weeks |
| Shipping + customs | 3-5 weeks |
| Inland transport (esp. to oil fields) | 1-3 weeks |
| Site install + commission | 3-5 weeks |
